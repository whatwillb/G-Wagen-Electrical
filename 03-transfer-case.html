Page 3: Transfer-case motor, connector, and feedback

Your transfer-case codes point to missing position data and an implausible direction-sensor signal. Inspect the motor connector and harness before buying the motor.

What you are trying to prove

  • Is the motor connector clean, dry, and tight?
  • Is the harness damaged near the transfer case?
  • Does commanded HIGH / LOW match actual position in live data?
  • Does the dash still go blank in the square?
A motor assembly can test "low voltage" simply because the connector or ground path is poor. Inspect first, replace second.

Step-by-step

  1. Safely raise and support the vehicle.
  2. Locate the transfer-case shift motor and unplug the connector.
  3. Inspect for oil intrusion, corrosion, spread pins, damaged locking tab, or harness rub-through.
  4. Clean the connector with electrical contact cleaner and reconnect firmly.
  5. Clear transfer-case and related transmission codes.
  6. Run the proper HIGH / LOW shift procedure and monitor live data.
  7. If actual position remains unavailable, or the indicator still goes blank, plan for motor replacement.
